---
title: how to write a brief in five minutes
date: 2026-09-12
excerpt: You do not need a forty-page document to start a project. Five short answers are enough for an accurate estimate.
---

A good brief is not long. It is clear. Most of the useful information fits in five answers, and you probably already know all of them.

## 1. What are you making?

One sentence. "A website for our dental clinic." "A booking app for a yoga studio." "A logo and colours for a new coffee brand."

## 2. Who is it for?

Describe the person you want to reach, not a demographic. "Busy parents who want a Saturday appointment" is far more useful than "ages 25 to 45".

## 3. What should it do for you?

Pick the one result that matters most: more enquiries, more bookings, fewer support calls, looking credible for investors. Everything else follows from this.

## 4. What do you already have?

A logo, photos, written content, an old website, a domain, a payment account. Each thing you already have makes the project faster and cheaper.

## 5. When do you need it, and is there a budget in mind?

A real date ("before our launch on the 15th") and a rough range help us suggest the right scope. It is fine if you do not have a number yet. That is what the estimate is for.

## What you can leave out

- Technical choices. That is our job.
- Long lists of features "for later". Write them down separately; we will plan for them without building them now.
- Perfect wording. Bullet points are great.

## Try it

The home page asks you these questions one at a time and gives you an estimate at the end. It only takes a few minutes.
